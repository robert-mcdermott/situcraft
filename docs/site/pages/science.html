How methods are checked

Three kinds of check, as each method allows: the same numbers as an independent implementation of the same method, on the same inputs (scanpy, SciPy, Squidpy and the like, run by the validation suite and recorded in the repository); the truth of a simulated tissue, whose cells, transcripts, types, domains and image placements are known; and invariants worked by hand on small cases. The heaviest checks run every week. The same inputs and settings give the same bytes on any computer, so a result recorded once is checked again exactly.

Bundles and the tissue

SituCraft reads every file of a Xenium bundle with its own readers: Parquet, Zarr, HDF5, TIFF and OME-TIFF with JPEG 2000. Every Parquet column of both development bundles reads as DuckDB reads it, every Zarr array as zarr-python and numcodecs read it, and every TIFF's structure and pixels as tifffile and imagecodecs read them. A bundle's check recomputes 22 of the run's recorded values from its data and reproduces each; thirteen bundles broken one way each raise exactly their findings. The Viewer draws every image sample as the bundle records it: channels read back from the page at several levels equal the derived data's tiles sample for sample. Quantiles in the QC plots are Hyndman and Fan's type 7, and box plots Tukey's.

Segmentation

Nucleus expansion gives each pixel to the nearest nucleus within the distance, by exact Euclidean distance transforms (Felzenszwalb and Huttenlocher 2012). It reproduces the Lung bundle's own 5 µm expansion: the same 11,898 cells, 98.1% of pixels and 98.9% of transcripts in the same cells.

Transcript-informed segmentation is implemented from the model of Jones et al. (2025): each cell a region whose transcripts are a Poisson process with its own rate for each gene, over a background, with expression states and cell sizes as priors; segmentations are sampled from the posterior and summarized, each transcript in its most probable cell with that probability kept, and convergence judged by Geweke's diagnostic. Every update is tested against its analytic posterior. On a simulated tissue it puts 84.7% of transcripts in their true cell, against the instrument's nucleus expansion's 82.2%; in patches it is as accurate (86.1% against 86.3% in one run). Its preflight estimated the memory of a whole run of the Lung example within 5%.

Comparing segmentations pairs cells that share a nucleus, or of which half of one lies in the other, and classes each difference. Two measures read transcripts given to the wrong cell: the spurious co-expression of gene pairs relative to nuclei alone (Jones et al. 2025), and the mutually exclusive co-expression rate of markers of different lineages (Hartman and Satija 2024). On the Lung example, errors planted for practice are found exactly: 60 planted, 60 found, each in its class.

Cell types

Clusters follow scanpy's defaults (Wolf et al. 2018): each cell scaled to the median total and log-transformed, each gene scaled, 50 principal components (computed exactly, or by the Lanczos method for panels above 1,000 genes), 15 nearest neighbors (by NN-Descent, Dong et al. 2011, above 50,000 cells), and Leiden communities (Traag et al. 2019) of modularity with a resolution (Reichardt and Bornholdt 2006). Markers are the rank-sum test's (Mann and Whitney 1947). Against scanpy, leidenalg, umap-learn and scikit-learn on both bundles and the simulated tissue: components within 10⁻¹³, the same neighbors, markers' scores identical to the rank-sum test with exact ties, and on the simulated tissue the clusters agree with the true types (adjusted Rand index 0.95; Hubert and Arabie 1985).

The map is UMAP (McInnes et al. 2018) from a spectral start, its faithfulness measured as trustworthiness (Venna and Kaski 2001): the same as umap-learn's of the same graph, by every measure within its spread over five seeds.

Labels from markers score each cell's markers against control genes of similar expression (Tirosh et al. 2016), as scanpy's score_genes does: every control set scanpy's, every score within 3 × 10⁻⁷, and 97% of the simulated tissue's cells their true type. Labels from a reference correlate each cell with each label's profile and fine-tune among the leading labels, as SingleR does (Aran et al. 2019): given the same profiles, every correlation within 3 × 10⁻⁸ and the same label on 99.97% to 100% of cells. The review queue finds at least 92% of the mislabels planted in the Breast section.

Pathology images

Landmark fits are least-squares similarities (Umeyama 1991) or affine transforms, each landmark with its residual and its leave-one-out error (Stone 1974). Automatic registration separates an H&E's hematoxylin by color deconvolution (Ruifrok and Johnston 2001), finds the scale and turn from log-polar spectra (Reddy and Chatterji 1996) and the shift by phase correlation (Kuglin and Hines 1975), keeps the candidate of highest normalized mutual information (Studholme et al. 1999), refines it by compass search (Hooke and Jeeves 1961), matches patches with sub-pixel phase correlation (Foroosh et al. 2002), and fits a smooth cubic B-spline deformation (Rueckert et al. 1999) when it predicts held-out matches better. On the published section, without their alignments, the H&E and the immunofluorescence image are placed within 0.61 and 0.68 µm of 10x's alignment; on simulated images, within 0.2 µm of the truth, and each checked patch's error is measured within 0.15 µm of its true one. Regions go to and from QuPath (Bankhead et al. 2017) in its GeoJSON: written for QuPath 0.7.0, they open there as written.

Where a question tests many pairs, labels or genes, its p-values are adjusted by Benjamini and Hochberg's procedure (1995). Under another segmentation, on the simulated tissue, the enrichment of its types and its niches hardly change between its true cells, the instrument's and the refinement's (rank correlation 1.00), while the markers' Moran's I nearly doubles under the instrument's expansion and stays within 0.03 of the truth's under the refinement. At a million generated cells, on four cores, each graph takes seconds and Moran's I with its permutations 88 s in 0.94 GB.

Cite SituCraft

If you use SituCraft in your research, please cite it (the repository's CITATION.cff), with its version, the methods you used as its methods section lists them, and a result's certificate by its fingerprint:
